Crossing rivers, building bridges

In the rural villages of Cagayan Valley, a young girl crossed rivers barefoot every day to get to school, carrying her books and lunch above her head. That girl was Dona Taguinod Leach, and the resilience she learned then became a mission to help others cross their own rivers of hardship.

When the pandemic left many Filipino students without family income and out of school, Dona, by then living in Australia, decided to act. In 2021 she launched the ISA Scholarship Program through ISA Australia. It began with five students in partnership with the Department of Education.

Today it combines financial aid, mentorship, pastoral care and leadership development, so students stay in school and thrive. It is not charity. It is a bridge, built on resilience, compassion and shared responsibility between schools, families and communities.

“Every scholar represents a dream rekindled, a future reshaped, and a community one step closer to upliftment.”
Dona Taguinod Leach, Founder and Managing Director

A 10-point policy framework

It turns the program from financial aid into a shared responsibility between schools, families, communities and alumni, with safeguards built in.

  1. 1

    Fair and transparent selection

    Students are chosen through a clear, merit-based process that balances academic ability with financial need, so scholarships go to students who are both high-achieving and genuinely underprivileged.

  2. 2

    Structured mentorship by educators

    Principals and teachers act as formal mentors, with regular check-ins that cover schoolwork, life skills and encouragement.

  3. 3

    Safeguards against misuse of funds

    School leaders sign agreements that scholarship funds are used only for the student's education.

  4. 4

    Parent and guardian partnership

    Families agree in writing that stipends cover school needs such as transport, uniforms, meals and learning materials, and take part in the scholar's pastoral care.

  5. 5

    Community validation

    Local community leaders issue a Certificate of Indigency to confirm a student's financial hardship.

  6. 6

    Ongoing monitoring and term reports

    Each term, students and mentors report on academic performance, school activities and any challenges, so ISA can step in early.

  7. 7

    Development and leadership plans

    Each scholar builds a personal academic and leadership plan with their mentor, with measurable goals.

  8. 8

    Program accountability and transparency

    ISA publishes annual reports on scholar progress, fund allocation and overall impact.

  9. 9

    Alumni and peer mentorship

    Graduated scholars stay connected as peer mentors and role models for the next generation.

  10. 10

    Holistic well-being and safeguarding

    Mentoring includes checks on emotional health, safety and personal development, with referrals to support services when needed.
